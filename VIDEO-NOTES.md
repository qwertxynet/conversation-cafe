# The Emirati Majlis: notes on the videos

complete captions on YouTube under "Show transcript". Times are mm:ss within each video.

## How the videos work
- **The narration seems to be in English, and the Arabic captions are a translation.** Some English lines say
  "A gift in Arabic is called *Hadiya*" and "coffee, in Arabic, *Gahwa*". The Arabic captions drop those words,
  which they would only need to do if the speech were English. The greetings on screen are spoken in Arabic.
  - For an Arabic-only room, the embedded player can switch on the **Arabic captions** automatically.

## Ep 1: Introduction & Greetings
- **السَّنَع**: the code of Emirati manners that guides how people behave with each other.
- Greeting exchange (1:14): السلام عليكم ← وعليكم السلام ورحمة الله · كيف الحال · حيّاك · مرحبا
- Greetings depend on the situation and come from religion and culture. Warm welcomes include a handshake,
  **التخاشم / المخاشمة** (touching noses, a sign of chivalry, pride and respect) and kissing an elder's head.
- At the door: the **host stands on the right side of the entrance** and leaves room for guests to pass.
- **The guest on the right goes in first**, but elders and **ذوو الهمم** (people of determination) take
  priority.
- **Shoes come off** and are set aside neatly, unless the host says to keep them on.
- Ends with: مرحباً، تفضلوا

## Ep 2: Majlis Etiquette
- Nobody rushes in: guests walk in slowly.
- People already in the majlis **stand up** to greet whoever arrives.
- Greet **starting from the right**, giving elders priority.
- The host shows guests where to sit. If the host is busy, **sit near other people** rather than alone,
  and ideally near the refreshments so serving is easy.
- Dress decently: the Emirati **كندورة**, or long trousers and a buttoned shirt.
- **Don't cross your legs so that a foot points at someone's face.**
- **Put your phone away.** Step out if a call is important.
- "The majlis is a place to laugh and joke", so the rules aren't meant to make it stiff.

## Ep 3: Historical Traditions of the Majlis
- What a majlis is: a sitting place where people **exchange news, discuss local events and issues, and socialise**.
  It is also used for weddings and condolences (العزاء).
- **Who may come in?** Everyone: family, friends, neighbours, travellers and strangers.
- For **sailors and pearl divers**, the boat itself was the majlis. They talked about the weather, ships,
  fishing, diving and the hardships of the sea.
- The **portrait of Sheikh Zayed** (the UAE's founder) hangs in the majlis to keep his memory alive.
  It is not the host.
- Arabs valued the guest highly even before Islam. The Gulf is a "high-context culture" that relies on
  non-verbal cues.
- **Story (2:25):** a man who wanted to marry but couldn't afford it put a **tray of sweets** in the middle
  of the majlis. The men understood: each took a sweet and left money in its place.
  - This is a good story to retell in session 3.

## Ep 4: The Art of Gift-Giving
- **هدية** (gift). It is good to bring something when you visit, and what you bring depends on who you're
  visiting. There are three scenarios:
  1. **Visiting with a big group or delegation, and you don't know the host:** a good book, a company
     memento, or a souvenir from your country. Avoid alcohol, pork and anything inappropriate.
  2. **A colleague's or friend's house:** something personal, linked to their background or hobbies.
  3. **An important person:** avoid generic or novelty gifts. Make it personal and thoughtful.
- If you run out of ideas: flowers, fine food or sweets.
- **The host may open the gift in front of you**, as a sign of respect.
- **Give and receive with the right hand, or both hands. Never hold out the left hand alone.**

## Ep 5: Coffee Customs (القهوة)
- Coffee stands for the **generosity and authenticity** of Emirati culture. It came through trade from
  **Yemen and Ethiopia**.
- It is spiced with **الهيل، الزعفران، القرنفل** (cardamom, saffron, cloves). No special occasion is complete
  without a cup.
- **القعقعة**: the rattle of the pot is the signal that coffee is ready to serve.
- **Take the cup with your right hand.** It's customary **not to refuse the first cup**.
- Traditionally **the youngest of the hosts serves**. Start with the **oldest or most important person**,
  or from the **right side** of the majlis if there's no elder.
  - The server is called **المقهوي**.
- **Raise the cup** in front of you to ask for more. **Shake it right and left** when you're done, and make
  sure the server sees you.
- **Don't put the cup on the floor**, which is disrespectful. Wait for the server to take it.
- **Why only a little coffee in the cup?** One story says the host poured a little at a time to keep the
  coffee warm, keep guests longer, and keep the conversation going.

## Ep 6: Dining Etiquette
- **Wash your hands** first.
- Traditionally people **sit on the floor**. There are two good ways: knees bent, which makes it easy to
  reach and serve food, or Eissa's position, which makes it easy to stand up. If you can't sit on the floor,
  tell the host.
- Three dishes: **هريس، بلاليط، لقيمات**
- **Don't start eating until everyone has a plate.** Serve yourself or wait for the host to serve you.
- **If you eat with your hands, use the right hand.** It's fine to ask for cutlery.
- Relax and ask your host when you're unsure. Ends with **بالعافية**.

## Ep 7: Fragrance and Hospitality
- Leaving a good impression includes **smelling good**. Perfume, **العود** and **البخور** are part of daily life.
  On special occasions (العيد، الأعراس) the whole house smells of burning oud.
- **Oud wood** is one of the world's most expensive natural materials: a kilo can cost as much as a kilo of
  gold, or more. Oud is a symbol of **generosity and dignity**.
- The host **passes the الدخون (incense burner) round** to the guests. It's a way to **welcome people arriving
  and say goodbye to people leaving**.
  - Tell the host if you have allergies or breathing problems.
- Oud oil carries memories of a house or a person. **الطربوش**, the tassel on the kandura, was dipped in oud
  oil before long sea voyages so the traveller would remember home.

## What this means for the sessions
- **Session 1 bingo words straight from the videos:**
  - السنع، حيّاك، التخاشم، ذوو الهمم، الكندورة
  - هدية، القهوة، الهيل، الزعفران، المقهوي
  - هريس، لقيمات، العود، البخور، الدخون، الطربوش
- **Do/don't cards** come from eps 1, 2, 4 and 6: shoes, entering from the right, standing to greet, feet,
  phone, right hand, waiting for everyone to have a plate, and so on.
- **The session 2 coffee round** can follow ep 5 exactly:
  - youngest serves
  - eldest or the right side first
  - right hand
  - never refuse the first cup
  - raise the cup for more
  - shake it when done
  - never put the cup on the floor
  - the pot's rattle means "ready"
- **Session 3** can open with the sweets-tray story (ep 3) and use "who may enter a majlis?" as a topic card.
  The leave-taking segment can mention passing the dukhoon (ep 7) without lighting anything.
- **Quiz questions** come naturally from the coffee signals, the three gift scenarios, the three dishes,
  and why only a little coffee is poured into the cup.
